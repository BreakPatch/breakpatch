Privacy policy

Last updated 6 October 2026.

Oscar Ivan Tigreros Bolanos, trading as Breakpatch (“Breakpatch”, “we”) makes Breakpatch, a Mac app for testing websites. Here's what we collect, in plain words first. The full details are further down.

The short version

  • Your tests stay yours. In the free version they live on your Mac. We never see them, and nor do we see screenshots or saved passwords.
  • The AI runs on your Mac. Nothing you test is sent to an AI company.
  • The free version counts, it doesn't watch. It sends us simple totals, like "12 tests run today", with nothing that says who you are. You can turn it off with one switch.
  • The paid versions, when they come, know who has a seat. To check a licence we keep your work email, which Mac uses the seat, and the same simple totals.
  • Paying is handled by Paddle. We never see your card.
  • No tracking on this website. No cookies and no analytics.

Your choices

  • Turn off the counts in the free version: Settings → Privacy. Nothing is sent after that.
  • Ask what we hold about you, to fix it or to delete it: email support@breakpatch.dev.
  • Leave a team: your company's licence admin can free your seat at any time.

The details

The free version: exactly what's counted

The first time you open Breakpatch it tells you it counts, and you can press Turn off. Nothing is sent before you've seen that message.

After that, on each day you use it, the app sends one short message with: how many tests were created and how many were run, passed and failed since the last message; whether this is the first message today, this week, this month and ever (so we can count how many Macs use Breakpatch without knowing which ones); and the app version.

It never sends test names, website addresses, steps, screenshots, saved passwords, your name, your email, or anything that identifies you or your Mac. We add the numbers into totals for everyone and don't keep the message itself.

Your internet address is only used for a moment, to stop one address from sending too many messages. It's never stored. When you turn counting off, nothing is counted or sent, and anything waiting is dropped. Advanced users can also turn it off with BREAKPATCH_NO_USAGE=1. The code is open source, so anyone can check this.

We also see how many times each download was fetched, as counted by GitHub. The install command itself sends nothing.

Breakpatch Team: what the licence check keeps

The licence service at account.breakpatch.dev runs on Google Cloud in Europe. It keeps:

  • Your company's licence: the company name, the licence admins' emails, the plan, seats and machines bought and in use, dates and status. The licence key itself is never stored in readable form.
  • Each seat: the email of the person using it, or a runner or CI machine's name; a scrambled code for the Mac it's used on, which can't be turned back into anything about the Mac; the ID of your company's workspace; the app version; and when the seat was taken, last used and freed. A seat nobody uses for 30 days is freed.
  • Simple totals with each check, about once a day: how many tests were created, how many runs there were and how many passed or failed, and on how many days Breakpatch was used. Only numbers. Your licence admins and Breakpatch can see them. They're part of how the licence works, so Team has no switch for them.
  • A log of licence changes: seats taken and freed, admin changes and payments, with who made them.
  • Back office sign-in: you sign in with a link sent to your email. Google's Firebase sends the email and keeps your email address and when you signed in.

To stop abuse, the service briefly counts requests per internet address and per key tried. It never stores the address in readable form, and the counters are deleted within a day or two. Google keeps standard request logs, which include internet addresses, for a limited time. Our own logs never contain licence keys.

Hosted by Breakpatch: a workspace we keep for you

Once it's offered, Hosted by Breakpatch keeps a Team or Business workspace for you on Google Cloud in Europe. Your tests are encrypted on your Macs with a key that's made there and that we never have, so we store them but can't read them. We see only what the workspace needs to work: who is in the team, ids, dates, pass or fail and counts. If you host your workspace yourself, in your own Firebase project, we hold none of it.

Buying through Paddle

Paddle.com sells Breakpatch on our behalf (it's the Merchant of Record). When you buy, Paddle collects your name, email, billing address, payment details and any tax number, under Paddle's own privacy policy. We never see card details.

Paddle tells us your name and email, the company name you enter, what you bought (plan, seats, machines, billing period), whether it's paid, and Paddle's reference numbers. We keep those with your licence, and make that email the licence's first admin so you can sign in and get your key.

Nothing is on sale yet. Once a plan is, Paddle's checkout only loads when you press Buy on the pricing page.

When the Solo plan is offered: before its checkout opens, the pricing page sends our licence service only the domain of the email you typed (for example initech.com, never the whole address), to check whether Solo can be bought for your company: Solo is one per company, and not for companies that already use Breakpatch Team or Business. Subdomains count as their company (eng.initech.com is initech.com). The answer is only whether that company already has Solo, or Team or Business (without saying which of the two, or who), and anyone can ask it for any company domain, since the pricing page is public. Personal email domains, such as gmail.com, aren't looked up, so nobody can learn whether a personal address has a licence.

This website

breakpatch.dev is hosted by Google's Firebase. It sets no cookies and runs no analytics. Its fonts come from Google Fonts, so your browser asks Google's servers for them.

The home, pricing and docs pages ask our licence service whether the Solo plan is on sale, and the home page also asks how many tests have been run with Breakpatch. The answers are the same for everyone, and nothing about you is sent with the questions. Your browser keeps the Solo answer (one setting in its local storage), so the next page shows right away.

Who handles data for us
  • Google (Firebase, Google Cloud): the website, the licence service and back office sign-in.
  • Paddle: checkout, payments, invoices, tax and refunds.
  • GitHub: app downloads and their counts, and issues you open there.
How long we keep it

Licence records, seats, usage totals and the change log are kept while the licence exists, and afterwards: billing and payment records for 6 years, as tax law requires, and seats and usage totals for 12 months. The free version's totals contain nothing personal and are kept.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of what we hold about you, ask us to correct it or delete it, or object to how we use it, by emailing support@breakpatch.dev. You can also ask to receive your data in a format you can take elsewhere, or ask us to limit how we use it. We answer within one month.

We use this data to provide the licence you or your company bought (a contract), and to keep the service safe and understand how it's used (our legitimate interests). If you're unhappy with how we handle your data, you can complain to the UK's Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk. We'd rather you told us first, so we can put it right.
